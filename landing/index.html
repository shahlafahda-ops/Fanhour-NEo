01 — The Regional Disconnect

The stadium has a ceiling.
The economy does not.

Regional clubs face a hard physical limit. Stadiums often max out between 3,000 and 5,000 seats, so every commercial model built on matchday attendance inherits that same ceiling. Sell every ticket, every match, all season — the revenue line still flattens.

Yet right outside the stadium walls, regional commerce is booming. Markets like Qassim and Asir move billions of Riyals a year through thousands of businesses whose customers are, quite literally, the same people in the stands.

02 — The Barrier to Entry

Sponsorship priced
for everyone but them.

Local SMEs — clinics, pharmacies, cafés, retailers — drive this regional wealth, and they genuinely want to support their clubs. It is their city, their team, their customers in the stands.

But traditional sports sponsorship was designed for national brands. A perimeter board package can run to hundreds of thousands of Riyals. That single number decides who gets to belong — and it forces capital out of the community and into national ad platforms, quietly disconnecting the club from the economy that surrounds it.

03 — Breaking the Walls

Fanhour transfers the club's
most valuable asset to the phone.

Fan loyalty was always the real inventory — it was just trapped inside a 5,000-seat building for ninety minutes a fortnight. We move it to the device every supporter already carries, then take the massive, exclusionary sponsorship package and slice it into affordable digital tiers.

No physical ceiling

Reach is no longer bounded by seats. Every supporter in the region — and every one who moved away — is reachable, every week of the season.
